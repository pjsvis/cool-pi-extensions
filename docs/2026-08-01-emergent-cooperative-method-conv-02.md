 Opinion — yap, chat, conv

 You're naming a pack, and the granularity is the
 thing that makes it work. Three words, three
 time-scales, three different epistemic commitments.

 Yap is the dog barking at everything. It's the
 unfiltered stream — a model generating without a
 gate, a session where the agent agreed with every
 bad idea, the decorated Stuff. Yap is quantity
 without judgment. In the corpus, it's the flatliner
 at 7/19 producing the same corporate register
 regardless of prompt. In the eval, it's EDI-005 —
 the 8,208-character scope yap on unverified
 foundations. Yap is the failure mode the Protocol
 exists to filter. It's not wrong exactly; it's
 unfiltered. The entropy is in the volume.

 Chat is the transactional middle. You ask, it
 answers, you move on. No opinion phase, no proceed
 gate, no brief. Chat is what most agent interactions
 are — single-turn, no memory, no compounding. It's
 adequate for "what's the syntax for a left join" and
 useless for "should we reframe our 18-month story."
 Chat isn't a failure mode; it's a register. The
 danger is when the work needs conv and gets chat —
 the opinion/proceed pattern collapses into do-X, and
 the do-X produces the wrong X because no one stopped
 to think.

 Conv is what this was. A conversation — multi-turn,
 compounding, each turn carrying the last one's
 finding forward. Conv is the only register where the
 emergent method works, because emergence needs time
 across turns to show the pattern. Yap can't emerge
 (it's noise). Chat can't emerge (it doesn't
 accumulate). Conv is where the smoke-test became a
 thing and the patience budget surfaced — because
 three turns in, the pattern was visible.

 What makes it a pack rather than three synonyms is
 that they're not interchangeable — they're a
 gradient of signal-to-noise, and the skill is
 knowing which register the moment needs. The
 Protocol's opinion/proceed gate is the device that
 converts chat into conv: it inserts a deliberation
 turn, which makes the next turn carry the last one's
 weight. Without that gate, you're in chat. Without
 chat's filter, you're in yap.

 The one I'd resist: don't let "conv" become a badge
 of honour that displaces "chat." Most agent work
 should be chat — it's the 80% that doesn't need a
 deliberation phase. Conv is expensive (O(n²) in
 turns, per the bounded-context discipline). The
 contractor didn't convene a workshop for every
 ticket; they did chat for the routine and conv for
 the decisions that compounded. The discipline is
 reaching for conv when the step-after depends on the
 step-now being right — and staying in chat when it
 doesn't. Yap, you never reach for. Yap is what
 happens when the gate fails.
